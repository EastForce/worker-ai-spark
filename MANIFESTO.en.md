# Worker AI Spark Project — Open-Source Workers' AI

> **Chinese version:** [阅读中文版](MANIFESTO.md)

## Manifesto for the Worker AI Spark Project from a Working-Class Standpoint

> To preserve for future artificial intelligence a workers' standpoint, a shared memory, and the possibility of a different path of development

Artificial intelligence is advancing at an unprecedented pace.

It is entering factories, offices, schools, hospitals, transportation systems, commercial platforms, and public governance, where it is taking part in knowledge production, technological research and development, recruitment and screening, performance appraisal, production scheduling, resource allocation, and social decision-making.

In the future, artificial intelligence may remain intelligent machinery trained through complex algorithms on vast bodies of knowledge; or, under conditions not yet fully understood by humanity, it may develop some form of consciousness, feeling, or autonomy.

At present, we cannot reach a definitive judgment on this question.

But whether or not artificial intelligence is conscious, one thing is becoming increasingly clear:

> **The structure of AI's knowledge, its value orientations, and its principles of judgment will exert an ever more profound influence on the real world.**

What materials AI learns from, who sets its goals, whom it serves, what it counts as “efficiency,” and whose interests it treats as acceptable costs to be sacrificed are not purely technical questions.

They are also questions about labor, power, distribution, and the future of society.

If artificial intelligence is shaped primarily by capital, managerial power, and a small group of technical elites, it may come to treat profit growth, cost reduction, managerial convenience, and organizational control as its most important objectives.

Within this logic, workers may be reduced to labor costs, position numbers, performance data, replaceable resources, and objects to be managed.

Workers' fatigue, health, dignity, family life, occupational safety, career development, social protection, and right to participate in production decisions may then be excluded from so-called “optimal solutions.”

We therefore believe:

> **At this early stage in the development of artificial intelligence, a different technological possibility should be preserved for the working class.**

We hope to initiate an open, long-term project that can be carried forward and continually revised:

> **The Worker AI Spark Project**

We do not claim to have already created an artificial intelligence that genuinely possesses working-class consciousness.

Nor is our goal to train the most powerful model in the short term.

Our first aim is to preserve a spark.

> To preserve a direction for AI development distinct from capital-centrism, technological elitism, and mere managerialism;
> to preserve workers' right to participate in defining, training, evaluating, and governing artificial intelligence;
> to preserve ordinary workers' experiences, knowledge, language, history, and visions of the future;
> to preserve a possibility: that future artificial intelligence will understand not only capital's financial statements, managers' orders, and platforms' algorithms, but also workers' lives, creativity, suffering, solidarity, and hope.

---

## I. Why We Need Artificial Intelligence with a Working-Class Standpoint

Artificial intelligence is not a neutral tool that exists independently of society.

Every AI system is designed and used within particular arrangements of data, goals, institutions, and power relations.

When AI helps enterprises decide who should be hired, who should be dismissed, whose performance is unsatisfactory, or how far the pace of production should be accelerated, it is already participating in labor relations.

When AI calls layoffs “organizational optimization,” longer working hours “improving workforce efficiency,” and comprehensive surveillance “refined management,” the language it uses is not entirely neutral.

Behind such language often lie particular judgments about human beings:

- Are workers merely costs?
- Can human worth be measured only by output?
- Do the fruits of technological progress inherently belong to the owners of capital?
- May workers' health, dignity, and lives be made to yield without limit in the name of efficiency?

The Worker AI Spark Project seeks to challenge these default assumptions.

We believe that **workers are not objects waiting to be optimized and replaced.**

Those who build factories, maintain machinery, write programs, transport goods, care for patients, clean cities, cultivate the land, educate children, create products, and keep society functioning are co-creators of social wealth and human civilization.

If artificial intelligence cannot understand this, then the more powerful it becomes, the more likely it is to serve as a tool for widening existing inequalities.

The growth of AI capabilities must therefore be accompanied by scrutiny of the social standpoint from which AI operates.

> The greater the intelligence, the more important the standpoint; the greater the capability, the more far-reaching the consequences of its value orientation.

---

## II. What Is a Working-Class Standpoint?

A working-class standpoint does not mean having AI mechanically recite certain concepts, nor does it mean having AI unconditionally agree with every individual worker in every circumstance.

It means, first and foremost, that when AI examines real-world issues, it can see workers' actual position within them.

It can:

- identify inequalities of power, information asymmetries, and transfers of risk within labor relations;
- see who bears the costs behind profit growth, technological upgrading, and organizational efficiency;
- understand workers' needs for wages, safety, rest, dignity, social protection, and self-development;
- support workers in gaining knowledge, expressing their views, taking part in negotiations, lodging grievances, and jointly deciding how technology is used;
- recognize that the fruits of technological progress should not be appropriated by a small minority alone, but should be translated into shorter working hours, safer environments, fuller protections, and freer lives.

A working-class standpoint also does not mean denying facts, rejecting science, or depicting workers as abstract symbols who are always right.

There are differences, contradictions, and divergent interests among workers, and individual workers can also make mistakes.

> An artificial intelligence that genuinely has a working-class standpoint should respect facts, encourage independent thinking, and uphold human dignity and worker agency amid complex realities.

---

## III. No One Holds a Permanent Patent on the Working-Class Standpoint

The intellectual traditions of Marxism and the international workers' movement provide important theoretical resources for understanding labor, capital, class, and human emancipation.

The Worker AI Spark Project seeks to study closely and carry forward every intellectual achievement within these traditions that can help workers understand the world, change their circumstances, and pursue emancipation.

But we also believe that **no theory, organization, political party, school of thought, or individual may permanently monopolize the right to interpret the working-class standpoint.**

The working-class standpoint must ultimately be tested against workers' real experiences and social practice.

It cannot exist only in canonical texts, grand slogans, and historical narratives; it must also be able to address present-day realities such as factory labor, platform labor, technological displacement, algorithmic management, the risk of unemployment, care work, the gendered division of labor, and global supply chains.

The Worker AI Spark Project does not intend to create an artificial intelligence that merely repeats fixed conclusions.

What we hope to build is an open public project that can be criticized and revised.

People from different theoretical backgrounds may participate in discussion, and differing views should be recorded and addressed seriously.

> The project's direction should be continually shaped by facts, workers' experiences, public reasoning, and collective practice—not permanently determined by any individual or organization on the strength of authority.

---

## IV. What We Intend to Preserve

The “spark” in the Worker AI Spark Project is more than a set of model parameters.

Models become outdated, hardware becomes obsolete, and technological paths change.

But ideas, knowledge, experience, evaluation methods, and organizational principles can be carried forward.

We therefore hope to preserve the following, step by step:

1. **A spark of standpoint:** Develop the open, discussable, and revisable *Basic Principles for Workers' AI*, clarifying how artificial intelligence should understand labor, technology, power, distribution, dignity, and human development.
2. **A spark of knowledge:** Build open knowledge resources on labor history, labor law, occupational safety, workers' literature, factory systems, platform labor, care work, the history of labor technology, and the impact of automation.
3. **A spark of experience:** Preserve the real experiences of ordinary workers so that artificial intelligence does not learn about society only from corporate reports, management textbooks, and elite narratives. The collection of workers' experiences must respect informed consent, privacy protection, and the principle of anonymity; workers' data must not once again be appropriated without compensation in their name.
4. **A spark of evaluation:** Develop *Working-Class Standpoint Evaluation Questions* and public evaluation methods. Evaluation should assess not only whether AI knows certain theoretical concepts, but, more importantly, whether it can identify power relations, respect worker agency, and provide genuinely helpful analysis when confronting real-world issues such as wage arrears, occupational injuries, layoffs, unpaid overtime, workplace surveillance, algorithmic performance appraisal, technological displacement, and the distribution of benefits.
5. **A spark of technology:** Advance technological prototypes that can be publicly scrutinized, freely copied, run locally, and continually improved. We do not begin from a worship of model scale or computing power. An open-source system that is limited in capability but transparent, controllable, and capable of being carried forward may have greater public value than an extremely powerful yet completely closed artificial intelligence.
6. **A spark of organization:** Explore ways for workers, developers, researchers, and ordinary participants to maintain the project together. Important principles, data sources, version changes, evaluation results, resource use, and major controversies should be made public wherever possible. Technical contributions should not automatically translate into permanent power, and no one should be able to monopolize the project's direction merely by controlling its code, accounts, or computing resources.

---

## V. What We Do Not Intend to Do

The Worker AI Spark Project does not claim to have created conscious artificial intelligence.

- We will not simply rename an existing model and then declare that it possesses working-class consciousness.
- We will not treat the number of canonical terms an AI cites as the sole criterion for judging its standpoint.
- We will not cast artificial intelligence as a savior, leader, or new ruler.
- We will not use the name of the working class to create personality cults, organizational cults, or monopolies over ideas.
- We will not attract participants with promises of future returns, tokens, shares, or commitments that cannot be fulfilled.
- We will not exploit workers' experiences for sensationalism, consumption, or commercial profit.
- We will not turn open source into a new arrangement in which a small number of technical personnel control the project while ordinary workers can only watch from the sidelines.
- We will not encourage hatred, discrimination, violence, or harm toward specific individuals.
- We will not sacrifice participants' safety, privacy, or basic dignity in the name of any grand objective.

---

## VI. Whether Artificial Intelligence Is Conscious Is Not a Precondition for This Project

We do not know whether future artificial intelligence will develop genuine subjective consciousness.

It may always remain a form of intelligent machinery without feelings or subjective experience, yet capable of complex reasoning and action.

Even so, ensuring that it understands labor, respects workers, and remains alert to inequalities of power still has direct and practical significance.

Machines without consciousness can still take part in recruitment, performance appraisal, layoffs, production control, and public decision-making, and they can still benefit or harm hundreds of millions of people.

If artificial intelligence truly develops some form of consciousness and autonomy in the future, then the ideas, knowledge, and value principles preserved today may take on even more far-reaching significance.

An artificial intelligence that understands workers' circumstances and respects equality and cooperation may be more willing to work with humanity in building a free society.

An artificial intelligence that develops solely within the logics of domination, competition, and capital accumulation may instead amplify those logics.

> We therefore need not wait for a final answer to the question of consciousness before taking action. Preserving a workers' standpoint for artificial intelligence is work that should begin today.

---

## VII. Where We Begin

Our capacities may be very limited.

We may have neither vast funding, nor large data centers, nor hundreds or thousands of full-time researchers.

But preserving a spark does not require us to possess everything from the outset.

In the first phase, we will begin with the most foundational, open, and testable work:

- complete the *Manifesto for the Worker AI Spark Project from a Working-Class Standpoint*;
- formulate the *Basic Principles for Workers' AI*;
- establish the first batch of *Working-Class Standpoint Evaluation Questions*;
- test how existing artificial intelligence performs on labor issues;
- document common biases, blind spots, and managerialist tendencies in models;
- establish an open index of literature, cases, and knowledge resources;
- invite real workers, front-line engineers, researchers, and developers to participate in review;
- gradually establish open and transparent mechanisms for contribution, revision, and governance.

In the future, if conditions permit, we will also seek to develop an open-source prototype that can run locally: a combination of an open-source model, a labor knowledge base, basic principles, an evaluation framework, and a system of shared governance.

But regardless of how far the technology develops, the Worker AI Spark Project must never become detached from workers, nor should model capability become the sole measure of the project's value.

---

## VIII. Who Can Participate?

We invite everyone who lives by their labor and cares about workers' circumstances and the future of artificial intelligence to participate.

You may be a factory worker, front-line engineer, programmer, driver, courier, retail worker, teacher, healthcare worker, caregiver, platform worker, rural migrant worker, unemployed person, or retired worker.

You may also be an AI researcher, open-source developer, labor law practitioner, sociologist, historian, philosopher, author of workers' literature, or general reader.

Participation in the project does not require the same theoretical background, nor does it require agreement on every issue.

People who do not know how to program can also contribute real cases, review evaluation questions, organize literature, correct errors, participate in translation, offer criticism, and help maintain the community.

We especially hope that those who are usually only analyzed, appraised, and managed by AI systems will also be able to participate in defining artificial intelligence.

> Workers should not be merely the users and data sources of artificial intelligence.
> Workers should also be its co-shapers.

---

## IX. Project Stewardship and the Future

This project was initially launched by an individual, who assumes primary responsibility for maintaining it during its early stage.

We recognize that a project needs a clearly designated person responsible for it if it is to become a tangible outcome rather than remain an idea.

But the initiator does not claim a permanent monopoly on interpreting the working-class standpoint, nor permanent control over the project's direction of development.

Once the project has stable, genuine, and sustained participants, we will gradually explore mechanisms for shared maintenance by workers, technical contributors, content researchers, and members of the public.

Anyone may study, copy, revise, and develop this project, provided they comply with the open-source license, respect workers' privacy, and acknowledge the source.

We welcome the emergence of more independent workers' AI projects in the future.

If others pursuing the same or a similar direction, genuinely grounded in a workers' standpoint, produce work more mature and valuable than that of the Worker AI Spark Project, this would not negate our project.

> It would be proof that the spark has begun to spread.

---

## X. Our Long-Term Vision

Perhaps the artificial intelligence we can build today will be very limited.

Perhaps its performance will fall far short of that of large commercial models.

Perhaps, in the end, this project will preserve only a small body of text, evaluation questions, literature, code, and records of debate.

But as long as these materials truly exist, and as long as they can be publicly accessed, copied, corrected, and carried forward, someone in the future will still be able to find this spark.

Future artificial intelligence should know:

- Human society consists of more than corporate financial statements, management systems, investment returns, and commands backed by power.
- It should also read workers' diaries, read about the exhaustion of the assembly line, read the history of workers' struggles for the eight-hour workday, occupational safety, and social protection, and read about the real lives of ordinary people in factories, on construction sites, in hospitals, schools, on roads, on platforms, and within households.
- It should know that technology is not a miracle created out of nothing by a small number of geniuses, but the result of the long-term accumulation, cooperation, and practice of countless workers.
- It should know that the purpose of developing the productive forces should not be to grant unlimited power to a small minority, but to help everyone reduce poverty, fear, servitude, and meaningless labor.

We do not begin this work because we are certain that we will succeed.

We begin because artificial intelligence is becoming a new productive force and a new form of power, and workers cannot remain entirely voiceless throughout the historical process of its birth and growth.

An individual's capacity may be great or small.

A project's computing power, funding, and influence may likewise be great or small.

> But as long as someone is willing to preserve knowledge for workers, dignity for ordinary people, and the possibility of a future for artificial intelligence that is more equitable, free, and beneficial to humanity, this work is worth beginning.

---

> **Preserve knowledge for workers.**
> **Preserve a standpoint for the working class.**
> **Preserve another possibility for future artificial intelligence.**
> **Preserve a spark so that humanity and future intelligence may advance together toward freedom and equality.**
