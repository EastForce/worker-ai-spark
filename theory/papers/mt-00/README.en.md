## Role of the General Paper

This article is the point of departure for the Worker AI Spark Project’s foundational theory. It does not reduce “human-centeredness” to generalized benevolence in model language. Instead, it defines the object of inquiry as the sociotechnical chain of power formed when AI enters labor relations and asks three connected questions: who may define technological objectives, who bears the costs of system errors and technical transition, and who has the practical power to correct a system?

The article introduces the goal–consequence mismatch as a middle-range concept connecting relations of production, goal encoding, the visibility of knowledge, the distribution of consequences, and institutional correction. It grounds a workers’ standpoint in knowledge of labor processes, exposure to risk, and the political standing of co-producers. It then limits power-sensitive alignment to a minimum governance structure consisting of goal legitimation, behavioral constraints, and the allocation of corrective powers. That structure is not a sufficient condition of workers’ emancipation and cannot substitute for changes in ownership, the distribution of gains, working time, or collective power.

## Public-Repository Reconstruction

The repository version preserves the academic-paper form: Chinese and English titles, abstracts, and keywords; continuous argument; conclusion; and all 36 references. Without reducing the density of the original argument, it has been reconstructed to:

- Limit claims about gaps in scholarship to the literature actually reviewed for the article;

## Evidentiary and Responsibility Boundaries

“Public discussion draft” means that the paper may be cited, criticized, and revised. It does not mean that the text is final, has passed formal peer review, expresses a consensus among workers or institutions, or has been validated through project practice. Its normative judgments, theoretical inferences, and candidate mechanisms should not be read as empirical results. Commitment to a workers’ standpoint also does not assume that actual workers are infallible: the paper requires factual verification, protection of third-party rights, recognition of differences among workers, and boundaries grounded in social reproduction, ecological sustainability, and the free development of all.

## Known Limitations and Questions for Discussion

- The four proximate mechanisms of the goal–consequence mismatch require comparative testing across forms of ownership, governance, and labor;
- The current literature base is not an exhaustive systematic review, and omitted scholarship may alter claims about novelty and scope;
- Translating the critique of the capitalist application of machinery into institutional analysis of a socialist market economy requires more specific study of public ownership, trade unions, collective bargaining, and public services;
- The conceptual boundaries among workers, unpaid caregivers, economically dependent self-employed people, unemployed people, and frontline managers require further refinement;
- The relations among openness, participation, evaluation, and a material transfer of power must be developed in subsequent thematic papers.
