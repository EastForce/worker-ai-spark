## Place in the Theory Series

This is the first thematic foundational-theory paper of the Worker AI Spark Project. It asks why workers cannot be treated merely as objects of AI governance, sources of data, or ordinary stakeholders, and why they instead possess standing as governance subjects in concrete deployments.

The paper reconstructs that standing through four institutional functions—rights, capabilities, voice, and remedy—which respond respectively to status being traded away, formal standing failing to become action, rules being formed unilaterally, and errors being impossible to correct. The four functions are a candidate normative and diagnostic framework that may be merged, expanded, or narrowed. They are not an exhaustive catalog of rights applicable across jurisdictions.

## Public-Repository Reconstruction

The repository version is based on the existing Word paper. It retains the original argumentative density, Table 1, all 14 source footnotes, and the academic-paper form. The reconstruction:

- Defines rights, capabilities, voice, and remedy as four institutional functions rather than a complete set of cross-jurisdictional legal duties;
- Retains the strongest objections based on legitimate management, genuine consent, external regulation, internal majorities, and absorption by ownership, strengthening the framework's falsifiability;
- Derives theoretical constraints for the Worker AI Spark Project without presenting future rights, participation, or correction mechanisms as present achievements;
- Defines the division of labor with later theories of rights bundles, case authorization, impact assessment, open-source governance, evaluation validity, and responsibility;
- Removes personal identity, affiliation, contact, and submission fields, and adds every independent source cited in the footnotes to the final reference list.

## Scope and Status

- The paper retains the familiar academic form of title, bilingual abstract, keywords, continuous argument, table, footnotes, conclusion, and references.
- It has not been validated through project practice or formal peer review and is not journal publication or academic certification.
- Cases and experiments cited in the paper illustrate possible mechanisms or feasible institutional arrangements; they do not establish universal causal effects for the four functions.
- The paper does not claim that the project has established stable worker participation, representation, review, or joint-governance institutions. It does not present an individual's account or an AI output as a worker consensus.

## Known Limitations, Major Objections, and Open Questions

- The literature base is not an exhaustive systematic review conducted under a preregistered protocol; omitted work may alter the classification and its boundaries.
- "Effective rights," "democratic accountability," or one principle of non-domination may perform the same tasks with fewer concepts. The four-part framework should be merged if doing so loses no diagnostic information.
- Genuine consent, low-dependence exit, or strong ex ante public regulation may reduce the need for direct worker voice in some settings; the relevant conditions require further comparison.
- The four functions have not become a measurement instrument tested for content validity, inter-rater agreement, or cross-group equivalence, and they have not been validated through project practice.
- Even full realization of all four functions may leave structural domination through ownership, benefit distribution, working time, and collective power intact.
