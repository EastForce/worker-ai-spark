# Foundational Theory and Core Papers

> **Chinese version:** [阅读中文版](README.md)

This directory is the public entry point for the Worker AI Spark Project's theoretical work. It explains the nature of the foundational theory, how it is developed, the boundaries of public claims, and its relationship to the core papers. Internal discussions, unauthorized identity information, restricted source material, and drafts not ready for release should not be placed here.

---

## Role of the Theoretical Work

The Worker AI Spark Project treats theory-building as part of the project itself. Its foundational theory is neither promotional text written to justify predetermined technical outcomes nor an unquestionable doctrine. It is a shared theoretical framework developed through literature research, conceptual analysis, and public argument, and it remains open to criticism, refutation, and revision.

The framework is intended to support work that:

- Understands the sociotechnical power structures created when AI enters labor relations;
- Explains why workers should be treated not only as users or affected persons, but also as subjects of knowledge, rights, and governance;
- Identifies possible structural misalignments among objective-setting, the inclusion of knowledge, the distribution of risk, and the capacity to correct errors;
- Establishes shared concepts that make the project's basic principles, knowledge of labor, evaluation design, technical requirements, and governance arrangements traceable to one another;
- Publicly records the scope of theoretical claims, objections, unresolved questions, and reasons for revision.

---

## Current Stage and Evidentiary Boundaries

The work is currently in the “literature research–theory formulation” stage. Its main activities are:

1. Finding and checking relevant literature;
2. Distinguishing empirical facts, theoretical inferences, normative judgments, institutional proposals, and unresolved questions;
3. Clarifying core concepts and their relationships to adjacent theories;
4. Formulating theoretical propositions for public discussion;
5. Addressing important objections, competing explanations, and conditions of applicability;
6. Preparing public discussion drafts in a conventional academic-paper format.

At this stage, the project does not report results from project practice, produce model rankings, claim that its theories have secured agreement among workers, experts, institutions, or formal peer reviewers, or describe a future “practice–discrepancy–revision” cycle as already completed.

---

## Formation and Evolution of the Theory

The formation and improvement of the foundational theory follows relationships of reasoning rather than a publicly promised timetable:

```text
Literature research and source verification
                    ↓
Conceptual analysis and problem reconstruction
                    ↓
Theoretical propositions, mechanisms, and boundaries
                    ↓
Public discussion drafts of the core papers
                    ↓
Cross-paper consistency review and public criticism
                    ↓
Future project practice and discrepancy records
                    ↓
Evidence-based theoretical revision and version evolution
```

“Literature research–theory formulation” describes the current work. Project practice, the identification of discrepancies, and practice-based theoretical revision are research procedures that may be undertaken later. Each public revision should preserve its version, evidentiary basis, principal objections, and unresolved disagreements.

## Structure of the Foundational Theory

The project plans to develop a system consisting of a general paper and a set of thematic foundational-theory papers:

- The **general paper** explains why the project needs to understand AI from workers' standpoints and defines the common object of analysis and basic criteria used across the framework;
- The **thematic papers** address workers' status as subjects, objective–consequence misalignment, labor experience and case rights, minimum rights, system design, impact assessment, open-source governance, evaluation validity, theoretical boundaries, chains of responsibility, and synthesis of the overall framework;
- Each paper should be readable on its own while identifying its theoretical premises, its division of labor with the other papers, and the conditions under which its claims may be challenged or revised.

The numbering, titles, and public versions are governed by the [core papers index](papers/README.en.md). Titles and drafts that have not been released do not count as completed theoretical outputs of the project.

## Open Theoretical Contributions

The project accepts papers, research notes, conceptual corrections, objections, and literature reviews without charging submission, review, or inclusion fees. Contributions may be produced independently by humans, through human–AI collaboration, or initially proposed by AI and then organized and verified by a human author. The project does not judge an idea by whether it first came from a human or an AI system, and it does not require human–AI collaboration to be specially highlighted; false claims about provenance or the use of AI are not acceptable. The human contributor who submits the work remains responsible for the argument, citations, facts, permissions, privacy, and subsequent responses.

The project evaluates theoretical work in light of truth-seeking, public criticism, and equality, asking whether it can help make the real world—and the design, use, and governance of AI—more equal. Contributions that are accepted or provide substantial help will be acknowledged in the repository's [contributor record](../CONTRIBUTORS.md) and on the project website according to the contributor's preferences. Contribution records will primarily recognize the humans who submit, verify, explain, and take responsibility for the work. This is not a journal, and inclusion does not constitute journal publication, formal peer review, or academic certification.

- Public discussion: [GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/EastForce/worker-ai-spark/discussions)
- Mature texts: submit a Pull Request following the [contribution guide](../CONTRIBUTING.md)
- Email submission: send the manuscript to the official mailbox, [worker.ai.spark@gmail.com](mailto:worker.ai.spark@gmail.com), with a subject such as “`[Theory Submission] Title`.” This option is intended for people who do not use GitHub, need to settle attribution and licensing before publication, or want a privacy check before making a text public. See the [email-submission procedure](../CONTRIBUTING.md#四邮箱投稿) (currently in Chinese)
- Do not submit contact details, identification documents, unauthorized cases, or other sensitive material through a public channel

Email submissions are free and do not automatically constitute inclusion, peer review, or academic certification. Email is neither anonymous nor end-to-end encrypted. The project must not publish an emailed manuscript or copy it into the repository until the author has explicitly confirmed the final text, public attribution, and CC BY-SA 4.0 authorization.

## Publication Status and Responsibility Boundaries

- A “public discussion draft” may be cited, criticized, and revised; the label does not mean that the paper is final or has passed formal peer review;
- Claims belong to the identified version of the paper and do not automatically represent workers, trade unions, experts, institutions, or organizations with which contributors are affiliated;
- Workers' experiences, case materials, names, affiliations, and original comments may be published only after separate, explicit authorization and a risk review;
- Citation errors, factual errors, conceptual conflicts, and omitted major objections should be corrected publicly, with necessary historical records preserved;
- Papers are covered by the repository's [CC BY-SA 4.0 content license](../LICENSE-CONTENT); the license text governs the applicable rights and obligations.
