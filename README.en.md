# Worker AI Spark Project — Open-Source Workers' AI

> **Chinese version:** [阅读中文版](README.md)

> The Worker AI Spark Project is an open-source initiative that seeks to preserve workers' perspectives, knowledge of labor, open evaluation methods, and mechanisms for shared governance in the development of future artificial intelligence.

## Why This Project Is Needed

Artificial intelligence is entering recruitment, performance evaluation, scheduling, production management, public services, and social decision-making. Regardless of whether AI ever truly develops consciousness, its growing knowledge structures, reasoning capabilities, and capacity for action mean that its value orientations and social standpoint will have real-world consequences.

This project is based on the following judgments:

- **Artificial intelligence is not inherently neutral.** It is shaped by particular data, objectives, institutions, and power relations.
- **Workers must not be reduced to objects to be managed or replaced.** When AI calls layoffs "organizational optimization," longer working hours "workforce efficiency," or comprehensive surveillance "refined management," the language it uses is not entirely neutral.
- **Workers should participate in defining and evaluating AI.** The people who build factories, maintain machines, write software, transport goods, care for patients, clean cities, cultivate land, and educate children should help shape AI, rather than serving merely as its users and sources of data.

This project seeks to preserve a direction for AI development that differs from a narrowly capital-centered, managerial, or technocratic approach.

## Current Project Status

Please note the project's current boundaries:

- This is the **initial official release (v0.1.0)**, and the project remains at a foundational stage;
- **No dedicated model has been trained**;
- **It has not been established that AI has developed consciousness**;
- The project's current outputs consist mainly of **principles, evaluation questions, and a governance framework**;
- An open-source prototype capable of running locally will be explored only if conditions permit in the future.

We do not claim to possess an AI with genuine working-class consciousness, nor do we aim to train the most powerful model in the short term. Our first aim is to preserve a spark.

## What the Project Is

The Worker AI Spark Project is:

- **An open research project**;
- **A public discussion project**;
- **An AI evaluation project**;
- **A project to preserve knowledge and experience of labor**;
- **A potential future open-source AI technology project** (still only an idea and not yet launched).

---

## What the Project Is Not

To avoid misunderstanding, this project is **not**:

- A completed large-scale AI model;
- A commercial investment project;
- An issuer of tokens, points, shares, or certificates of financial return;
- A promise of funding, jobs, computing resources, or economic returns;
- A representative of any workplace, school, organization, or institution with which a participant is affiliated;
- A source of a single conclusion on behalf of the "entire working class";
- An advocate of hatred, violence, or harm against specific individuals;
- A project that treats any individual, organization, model, or theory as an unquestionable authority.

## How to Participate

This project particularly hopes that people who are usually only analyzed, evaluated, and managed by AI systems can also participate in defining AI. Participation **does not require programming skills**.

You can:

- **Submit labor scenarios:** You may provide real cases that you personally experienced, directly learned about, or have reasonable factual grounds for. You may also submit scenarios drawn from public sources, composite scenarios, or hypothetical scenarios. You may choose to use your real name, a pseudonym, remain anonymous, or make the submission available only for internal handling; authorization to disclose identity and authorization to disclose the case itself are handled separately. GitHub account names are always visible, so anonymous or internal-only submissions must use a non-public channel to be announced later;
- **Review evaluation questions:** Assess whether they reflect reality and whether important issues have been omitted;
- **Identify factual errors:** Flag incorrect citations, broken links, or questions about the applicability of laws;
- **Submit literature:** Add public and lawfully accessible sources and research on labor;
- **Contribute translations:** Help the project reach people who use other languages;
- **Write code:** Work on data formats, evaluation scripts, local model execution, web presentation, or automated testing;
- **Test models:** Use the evaluation questions to examine the responses of existing models;
- **Improve governance:** Help refine the mechanisms for contribution, revision, and dispute resolution;
- **Review the basic principles:** Offer item-by-item comments based on professional research or real experience of labor;
- **Participate through Issues:** Knowledge of Git is not required; see [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).
